# Compendium Packs

[← Wiki home](Home.md)

Where the module keeps content it creates, and the rules it follows about your
data.

---

## The content contract

Four rules, applied everywhere:

1. **Every document-creating entry point is GM-only.**
2. **Nothing is overwritten silently.** Conflicts always prompt.
3. **Nothing is ever deleted.** The module does not call `deleteCompendium`, and
   the guided duplicate-cull only ever removes copies inside its own packs,
   never world documents, never `_Backup` documents.
4. **Re-importing is idempotent.** The same paste committed twice does not
   produce two copies.

Everything the module creates is an **ordinary Foundry document**. Disable or
remove the module and your content stays readable and usable.

## The packs

Packs are **world compendiums**, created on demand. Nothing exists until
something needs it. They live in the sidebar under a **Shadowdark Enhancer**
folder. The export/import bundle includes all suite packs, so curated items,
generated entries, and conflict state survive a world migration.

### Generated Artifacts and Replace-Always Boundary

Documents created by the module's generation pipelines inside the managed Items
pack (`world.shadowdark-enhancer--items` / `sde-items`) that carry the explicit
top-level generated marker (`flags["shadowdark-enhancer"].generated === true`)
are authoritative under the structural replace-always contract (A7/D6):
re-running their generator replaces the document in full, including art and
properties. Generated items outside the managed pack or ordinary imported items
obey standard provenance preservation rules instead.

Documents generated by the Monster Spell Library also live inside `sde-items`
(under `Monster Spells / <source>`) and carry
`flags["shadowdark-enhancer"].monsterSpell.generated`, but that marker does
**not** confer replace-always. Its refresh detects hand-edited generated
spells, reports them as curated conflicts, and preserves them — see
[Monster Spell Library](Monster-Spell-Library.md).

### The curated-icon resolver — weapons (D1) + armor (D2) + Basic Gear (D3) + Sea Wolf Plunder (D4) active

The module-owned Items pack (`world.shadowdark-enhancer--items`, plus any other `world.` pack the module owns — the allowlist is wider than A4's `MANAGED_ITEMS_PACK` by design) participates in a shared **curated-icon resolver** (`scripts/shared/curated-icons.mjs`, pure, Foundry-free; discovery in `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/index.mjs`). The generic mechanism — two key spaces, display-name-derived normalization, discovery by import, semantic-category-before-fallback ordering, `world.` target allowlist, and two-stage coverage/drift audit — was introduced in A4. **D1 activates the weapons map (37 rows), D2 the armor map (13 rows: 9 canonical + 4 mithral aliases), D3 the Basic Gear map (44 rows: 37 canonical + 7 quantity/spelling aliases), and D4 the Sea Wolf Plunder map (20 source-qualified rows under `cs3`).** The composed registry contains 94 bare rows + 20 sourced rows (114 total entries in registry), zero problems, and zero cross-map collisions.

**D1 weapons activation (37 rows).** The map `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/weapon-icons.mjs` is registered through `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/index.mjs` and consulted in `scripts/importer/items/item-importer.mjs:buildItemData` via `_automaticArt` → `curatedArtFor({name})`. On creation, any module-managed `Weapon` Item whose final name matches one of the 37 reviewed names receives its `icons/...webp` icon and is stamped `curated` (see `curatedArtFor` / `_artStampFor` and A3 provenance).

The map is source-agnostic: `curatedNameKey(finalDocument.name)` alone → `bastard sword`, so `Bastard Sword` resolves the same regardless of which supported book printed it. N3 verified gear names are globally distinct across supported books, so one mapping applies to every copy. Together with D2 armor and D3 Basic Gear, the bare-space registry holds 94 rows (37 weapons + 13 armor entries counting aliases + 44 Basic Gear entries counting aliases) with no collisions.

**Full N3 weapons census (37):** `Bastard sword`, `Blowgun`, `Bolas`, `Boomerang`, `Chakram`, `Club`, `Club (Obsidian)`, `Crossbow`, `Dagger`, `Dagger (Obsidian)`, `Falchion`, `Greataxe`, `Greatsword`, `Handaxe`, `Javelin`, `Lance`, `Longbow`, `Longsword`, `Mace`, `Morningstar`, `Pike`, `Rapier`, `Razor chain`, `Sai`, `Scimitar`, `Shortbow`, `Shortsword`, `Shuriken`, `Sling`, `Spear`, `Spear (Obsidian)`, `Spear-thrower`, `Staff`, `Stave`, `Strikes`, `Warhammer`, `Whip`.

**Intentional semantic selections for three entries that had no distinct weapon pick:** `Lance` → `icons/skills/melee/strike-polearm-light-orange.webp`, `Morningstar` → `icons/skills/melee/strike-morningstar-gray.webp`, `Strikes` → `icons/skills/melee/unarmed-punch-fist-blue.webp`. Each sits in `icons/skills/melee/...` (the same semantic-category selection documented in N3, not a typo).

**D2 armor activation (13 rows: 9 canonical + 4 mithral spelling aliases).** The map `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/armor-icons.mjs` is registered through the same A4 discovery/index and consulted at the same call site (`buildItemData` → `_automaticArt` → `curatedArtFor({name})` → `_artStampFor`). On creation, any module-managed `Armor` Item whose final name matches one of the 13 reviewed armor display names receives its `icons/...webp` icon and is stamped `curated`. The map is source-agnostic (`curatedNameKey(finalDocument.name)` alone → `leather armor`, `mithral chainmail`; both spellings of a mithral armor resolve to the same reviewed path). Covers the full N3 armor census: 9 canonical — `Leather armor`, `Chainmail`, `Mithral Chainmail`, `Plate mail`, `Mithral Plate Mail`, `Shield`, `Mithral Shield`, `Round shield`, `Mithral Round Shield` — plus 4 deliberate source-spelling aliases sharing a reviewed path without inflating canonical inventory: `Chainmail, mithral` ↔ `Mithral Chainmail` → `icons/equipment/chest/breastplate-banded-steel-grey.webp`; `Plate mail, mithral` ↔ `Mithral Plate Mail` → `icons/equipment/chest/breastplate-cuirass-steel-blue.webp`; `Shield, mithral` ↔ `Mithral Shield` → `icons/equipment/shield/heater-crystal-blue.webp`; `Round shield, mithral` ↔ `Mithral Round Shield` → `icons/equipment/shield/round-wooden-boss-steel-yellow-blue.webp`. Remaining canonical paths: `Leather armor` → `icons/equipment/chest/breastplate-layered-leather-brown.webp`; `Chainmail` → `icons/commodities/metal/mail-chain-steel.webp`; `Plate mail` → `icons/equipment/chest/breastplate-layered-steel.webp`; `Shield` → `icons/equipment/shield/heater-steel-gray.webp`; `Round shield` → `icons/equipment/shield/shield-round-boss-wood-brown.webp`. All 9 distinct canonical icons plus the 4 alias-shared paths were chosen from the matching `icons/equipment/...` or `icons/commodities/...` semantic folder and vetted against the real Foundry icon tree. Armor, weapon, and basic-gear maps share the one `BARE` key space; N3 verified gear names are globally distinct and bare/alias provenance is case/whitespace/curly-quote normalized with punctuation preserved, so only the four deliberate alias pairs intentionally share a path.

**D3 Basic Gear activation (44 rows: 37 canonical + 7 quantity/spelling aliases).** The map `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/gear-icons.mjs` is registered through the same discovery index and uses the same source-agnostic bare-name seam. The seven aliases — `Arrows (20)`, `Caltrops (one bag)`, `Candle (3)`, `Crossbow bolts (20)`, `Iron spikes (10)`, `Rope, morzo silk`, and `Rations (3)` — retain distinct normalized keys while sharing the reviewed canonical icon path. Matching Basic Gear is stamped `curated`; untouched module-owned art can upgrade, GM-custom art is preserved, and an unmapped name keeps the generic `default` fallback.

**D4 Sea Wolf Plunder activation (20 rows).** The map `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/sea-wolf-plunder-icons.mjs` is registered in the sourced key space (`cs3`) through A4's discovery index. When materializing the *Cursed Scroll 3* p68 *Sea Wolf Plunder From Distant Lands* table into `world.shadowdark-enhancer--items`, each generated treasure item resolves its reviewed N3 §5.1 icon via `curatedArtFor({ name, source: "cs3" })` and is stamped `curated`. The map is source-qualified: lookup keys are `<sourceId>:<normalize(name)>`, preventing collisions with other books. Remaining treasure maps (D5 Dead Bandit Loot, D6 Diabolical Treasure) activate with their owning tickets.

**Provenance and upgrade rules (A3 + A4 — weapons, armor, and Basic Gear).** A fresh import with no draft art, an item still carrying the module default, an untouched previously imported image, and a prior `curated` pick are all upgradeable — a later map revision upgrades an untouched curated pick on re-import. Any image the GM has edited diverges from the `flags["shadowdark-enhancer"].art.img` witness and is classified `custom`, preserved and never overwritten. Enabling a map does not reclassify an already stored `imported` Item; its stored classification stands until the image itself changes. An unmapped weapon, armor, or Basic Gear name keeps the generic/type fallback (`default`) and an item outside the module-managed surface is unaffected.

**Armor coverage and drift gate — two-stage, per-category (D2).** Same two-stage contract as weapons: map construction (syntax) plus per-category gate against the real Foundry icon tree. `test/armor-icons.test.mjs` asserts the exact 13-row N3 census (9 canonical + 4 alias), the 9 distinct canonical keys, and the 4 alias pairs; `buildCuratedIconRegistry([ARMOR_ICONS])` reports `{total: 13, bare: 13, perMap: [{label: "armor", space: "bare", entries: 13}], problems: []}` structurally, and `auditCuratedIconRegistry(registry, {pathExists: (p) => foundryIcons.has(p)})` against the recursively indexed `FOUNDRY_ICON_ROOT` inventory (default `/home/patricks/FoundryV14/public/icons`, overrideable via `SHADOWDARK_ENHANCER_FOUNDRY_ICON_ROOT`) proves no `missing-path`; a valid-looking absent armor asset reports `missing-path`. Without the tree only the structural/census/provenance/alias assertions run.

**Basic Gear coverage and drift gate — two-stage, per-category and composed (D3).** `test/basic-gear-icons.test.mjs` asserts the exact 44-row N3 census, the 37 canonical keys, and the 7 deliberate aliases. It also audits the actual discovery registry rather than only the isolated map: `curatedIconRegistry()` reports 94 bare rows (37 weapons + 13 armor + 44 Basic Gear), zero sourced rows, zero problems, and zero cross-space names. The structural census always runs; when `FOUNDRY_ICON_ROOT` is available, the same audit checks all 94 accepted paths and a valid-looking absent asset produces `missing-path`. If the directory is unavailable, only those disk-backed assertions skip; an existing but incomplete inventory fails rather than passing vacuously.

**Weapon coverage and drift gate — two-stage, per-category (D1).** Map construction enforces native `icons/**.webp` syntax (`^icons/[^\s]+\.webp$`) and structural rules — a duplicate key, blank name, `unknown-space` or `unusable-source` never throws; the row is dropped into `problems` and that item keeps fallback art. Syntax alone does not prove the asset exists on disk. The second stage is D1's weapons gate: `test/weapon-icons.test.mjs` loads the real Foundry icon inventory when available and calls `auditCuratedIconRegistry(registry, { pathExists: (p) => foundryIcons.has(p) })`; a syntactically valid but absent weapon asset then produces the stable `missing-path` finding. `auditCuratedIconRegistry()` without `pathExists` remains structural-only. `problems` empty across both stages is the contract; `crossSpaceNames` is informational (a treasure name that also exists as gear resolves qualified-first). Without the Foundry tree only the structural census/provenance/fallback assertions run and the inventory checks skip — the gate still fails rather than vacuously passes when the inventory is incomplete.

How it is keyed — and why the split matters structurally:

* **Weapons, Armor, Basic Gear — bare keys.** `curatedNameKey(finalDocument.name)` alone (case-folded, whitespace-collapsed, curly-quote-folded `'`, punctuation preserved). The import-time image decision lives in `scripts/importer/items/item-importer.mjs:buildItemData` / `_automaticArt`, which structurally cannot know which book a draft came from: `source` is a commit-time batch option (`createItems(drafts, {source})`) and only afterwards appears as `flags[MODULE_ID].source` on the document. A gear key requiring the book would be unresolvable at the one place gear art is chosen. Gear names are globally distinct across supported books (N3 verifies this), so one mapping applies to every copy regardless of which book prints it.

* **Treasure/plunder — source-qualified keys.** `curatedSourcedKey(source, name)` → `<sourceId>:<normalize(name)>`, collapsing every spelling (`CS3`, `Cursed Scroll #3`, `Midnight Sun` → `cs3`; both Western Reaches guides → `wr`) through `sourceKey`. Treasure materializers *do* know which table they are draining — the book is part of their call — and treasure names are book prose generic enough to collide across Cursed Scrolls, so qualification is cheap and forward-proof. Raw table-row prices, feature text and display markup never enter the key; materializers must settle the final Item name before calling.

How it is written and registered:

* **Display-name-derived keys.** A map row is `"Display Name": "icons/…webp"`; the lookup key is computed at load. No hand-written normalized column sits beside the name — the source spec drifted that way (`…St. Terragnis…` un-lowercased) and a shape that makes the mistake unrepresentable is preferred over a test that merely catches it.

* **Discovery by import.** Each reviewed map is its own module under `scripts/shared/curated-icon-maps/` that calls `registerCuratedIconMap(label, rows, {space: CURATED_KEY_SPACES.BARE | SOURCED})` at import time. The index is side-effect imported once from `scripts/shadowdark-enhancer.mjs` before any item is built. Adding a D-ticket map is one file plus one `import "./<map>.mjs";` line — no shared array literal for tickets to collide on. `defineCuratedIconMap` / `buildCuratedIconRegistry` validate keys/paths; the bare space is shared by weapon/armor/gear maps, so a cross-map duplicate is a registry-level duplicate even when each map is internally consistent.

How it composes and degrades:

* **Semantic category before broad fallback.** `_automaticArt` composes `Spells (Shikashi channel)` → `TYPE_DEFAULT_IMG` for `Background`/`Talent`/`Ancestry`/`Class` (the item maps are name-distinct only among themselves — a Talent sharing a gear name must not inherit its icon) → `curatedArtFor({name})` → `pickTreasureIcon(name)`. The curated maps *are* the semantic layer — each entry chosen from the matching `icons/weapons/…`, `icons/equipment/…`, `icons/sundries/…` folder, fallbacks documented in N3. A hit returns `{img, artState: "curated"}` and is stamped via `_artStampFor`; the generic chain remains `default`. Both `curated` and `default` are upgradeable under A3, while `custom` (human edit) is never overwritten.

* **Unmatched returns `null`.** `resolveCuratedIcon` returning `null` means "I have no opinion" — the caller keeps fallback art. There is no guessing tier on purpose: a wrong curated icon looks deliberate.

* **Target boundary — `world.` allowlist.** `isCuratedApplyTarget(packCollection)` permits only `world.` packs. This is wider than A3's `MANAGED_ITEMS_PACK` (one exact pack for replace-always) and deliberately so: curated art may be written to any world pack this module owns content in, but never to `shadowdark.gear` or any other system pack. `LootLinker` resolves plunder rows system-pack-first by design, so a resolved `uuid` may point into the base system — one update call away from editing a compendium that must not be mutated (`Basilisk Egg`, `Holy Symbol`, `Crawling Kit`, obsidian weapons share curated names with base-system items).

* **Coverage/drift — total construction, audited in two stages (syntax, then existence).** A duplicate key, blank name, non-`icons/**.webp` *syntax* (`^icons/[^\s]+\.webp$`) or unusable source never throws; the row is dropped into `problems` and that item keeps fallback art. Syntax alone does not prove the asset exists on disk. The second stage is the per-category D1–D6 gate: each owning test loads the real Foundry icon inventory and calls `auditCuratedIconRegistry(registry, { pathExists: (p) => foundryIcons.has(p) })`; a syntactically valid but absent asset then produces a stable `missing-path` finding. `auditCuratedIconRegistry()` without `pathExists` is structural-only. `auditCuratedIconRegistry()` reports `{total, bare, sourced, perMap, problems, crossSpaceNames}` — `problems` empty across both stages is the contract; `crossSpaceNames` (the same prose name appearing in both bare and sourced spaces) is informational, not a failure — a treasure name that also exists as gear resolves qualified-first.

As with the provenance contracts above, **legacy unmarked current-map art remains upgradeable**: a legacy unmarked document wearing the map's current path classifies as `default` under A3 and stays upgradeable; removing a row later reclassifies it as `custom`, which is the safe direction for that change.

Composed D1+D2+D3+D4, weapons, armor, Basic Gear, and CS3 Sea Wolf Plunder are active; remaining treasure maps (D5/D6) activate with their owning tickets. The discovery-registry audit reports `{total: 114, bare: 94, sourced: 20, problems: [], crossSpaceNames: []}` and verifies every accepted path when the Foundry icon tree is available.

### Suite packs

| Pack id | Type | Label | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sde-actors` | Actor | Shadowdark Enhancer — Actors | Imported monsters |
| `sde-items` | Item | Shadowdark Enhancer — Items | Imported items, spells, gear, generated Monster Spells, and generated Sea Wolf Plunder treasure items |
| `sde-tables` | RollTable | Shadowdark Enhancer — Roll Tables | Imported tables |
| `sde-journal` | JournalEntry | Shadowdark Enhancer — Journals | *(structural)* |
| `sde-scenes` | Scene | Shadowdark Enhancer — Scenes | *(structural)* |

> **Monster Spells pack consolidation:** In earlier releases, generated monster
> spells were written to a separate `world.shadowdark-enhancer--monster-spells`
> compendium. Generated monster spells now live inside `sde-items` under
> `Monster Spells / <source>` (e.g. `Monster Spells / Shadowdark Core`,
> `Monster Spells / Cursed Scroll 3`). Any existing content in the legacy pack is
> automatically migrated into `sde-items` on activation by the primary GM
> (moving hand-authored GM content to `Monster Spells / Other Sources` and
> preserving curated edits and art). The retired pack is emptied and remains
> present for one release as a visible deprecation and compatibility shell
> before being removed. Because migrated documents receive new IDs inside
> `sde-items`, individual legacy `@UUID` references do not resolve and should be
> repointed to their counterparts in the Items pack. Re-running the migration is
> safe and idempotent.

### Character Options packs

Nested under a **Character Options** sub-folder, mirroring the system's own
grouping:

| Pack id | Label |
|---|---|
| `classes` | Classes |
| `talents` | Talents |
| `class-abilties` | Class Abilties |
| `spells` | Spells |
| `background` | Background |
| `ancestries` | Ancestries |
| `languages` | Languages |
| `patrons-and-deities` | Patrons and Deities |

> **The odd ids and the `Class Abilties` spelling are deliberate.** These packs'
> **labels slugify to their collection ids** (`Classes` → `world.classes`), so
> importing a bundle into a fresh world recreates the identical `world.<slug>`
> collection. That keeps every cross-pack `@UUID` reference valid:
> class → talent, spell → class, table → document, ancestry → talent. Renaming
> them to fix the typo would break links in every exported bundle. Two packs
> (Languages, Patrons and Deities) are carried empty purely so an imported suite
> mirrors its source exactly.

## Source folders

Inside each pack, content is filed into a folder named for the book it came from,
derived from the source label you commit with:

| You type | Folder |
|---|---|
| `cs1` … `cs6`, or `Cursed Scroll 1` … `6` | `CS1` … `CS6` |
| `pgwr`, `gmgwr`, `wr`, `Western Reaches` | `Western Reaches` |
| `core`, `Core Rulebook` | `CORE` |
| *(blank)* or `custom` | `Custom` |
| anything else | Upper-cased |

Both the short code and the full book name fold to the **same** folder. This
matters, because the Manage tree's census is keyed on the short form. An import
filed under the wrong label will show as a gap forever while a duplicate all-caps
folder sits beside it.

Specialized sub-folders are created under their respective source roots when
needed:
- **`Monster Spells / <source>`** inside `sde-items` for generated monster spells.
- **`Western Reaches / Weapon Properties`** inside `sde-items` for canonical Western Reaches weapon Property items — siege *Blast*/*Exploding* and the WR **Lance** triple *Charge*/*Devastating*/*Mounted* (B2/B5 `wr-property-importer` seam). Legacy root properties are moved into this folder in place on re-import without changing document UUIDs. Ordinary weapons and gear remain in their standard folder locations; only an exact `Lance` name materializes the WR triple.

## Link resolution

`@UUID` links resolve to the **system's own `shadowdark.*` packs first**, and
fall back to the module's world packs. Imported content prefers to point at the
content you already had instead of at a copy.

## Ownership

Players get **Observer** on **`sde-actors` only**, so monster `@UUID` links
resolve on player-facing sheets and cards. **Every other suite pack is GM-only**
(player ownership `NONE`): items, tables, journals, scenes, and all the
Character Options packs. Player-facing features (the builder, the merchant)
read that content through GM-mediated paths instead. Note that v14
compendium ownership maps user **role names** to ownership level **names** as
strings. Numeric levels are silently rejected, which is why you may see this
handled explicitly in the code.

## Moving content between worlds

Use the [Importer Hub](Importer-Hub.md)'s **bundle export/import** (Tools menu).
It writes the whole suite as one JSON file. On import it validates, **skips
anything that already exists, and never overwrites**, so it is a way to seed a
new world, not to sync two worlds. Pre-consolidation (format-1) bundles with a
legacy `packs.monsterSpells` payload are automatically restored into `sde-items`
under the `Monster Spells / <source>` folder hierarchy, reporting explicit
failure on errors rather than silently omitting documents.

Because pack ids are reconstructed from labels, a bundle imported into a fresh
world keeps all its internal links intact.

## Troubleshooting

**The packs don't exist.**
They are created on demand. Import something and they appear.

**Content landed in a `Custom` folder.**
You committed without a source label. Set the source before committing. The
Source dropdown offers Core Rulebook, Cursed Scroll 1–6, and Western Reaches.

**A duplicate all-caps folder appeared next to the proper one (e.g. `CURSED SCROLL 1` beside `CS1`).**
A source label was used that didn't fold to the expected short code. Move the
documents into the correct folder. Future imports with a recognised label will
file correctly.

**Links broke after importing a bundle into a new world.**
Check that the Character Options packs came across with their original labels.
The collection ids are derived from them. A renamed pack means a different
`world.<slug>` and dead links.

**I want to delete a pack.**
Do it yourself in the sidebar. The module will never delete a compendium, so
nothing is going to remove it for you, and nothing will object if you do.
